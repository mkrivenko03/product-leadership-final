# Outcome Roadmap & Trade-off Memo: Meridian Foundations

> Module 2 · Turn Your Strategy into an Outcome Roadmap

## 1. Trade-off memo

> **Rock 1:** Add a photo markup and annotation tool for field teams (supports KR1): Provide immediate value to foremen by allowing them to communicate and document field issues directly through annotated photos, reducing reliance on separate messaging tools.
>
> **Rock 2:** Simplify the daily log: reduce from 14 required fields to 4 (supports KR2): Reduce the reporting burden from 14 required fields to 4, making it more likely that field workers complete daily updates.
>
> **Rock 3:** Build an offline-first mobile experience for job sites with poor connectivity (supports KR2): Enable field workers to capture progress where connectivity is unreliable, storing updates locally and synchronizing them when a connection becomes available. This reduces reporting gaps caused by connectivity limitations and improves continuity of project status information.
>
> **Hard no 1:** Create a foreman-facing mobile app (separate from the PM web app): We will not build a separate foreman-facing mobile app. Instead, we will expand the existing Meridian mobile experience with offline capabilities and field-specific functionality, creating a single entry point for users. This avoids duplicating infrastructure, fragmenting workflows, and increasing maintenance costs while allowing us to incrementally validate and expand the value we deliver to foremen.
>
> **Hard no 2:** Build a subcontractor portal with document sharing: We will not prioritize building a subcontractor portal this quarter. While it could strengthen Meridian's enterprise offering, it introduces a substantial new workflow and additional complexity without directly addressing our immediate field-adoption and data-gap objectives.
>
> **Hard no 3:** Add AI-assisted RFI (Request for Information) drafting: We will not prioritize AI-assisted RFI drafting during the Foundations roadmap horizon. While it could improve productivity, it does not directly address our primary challenges of field adoption and incomplete project-status data.
