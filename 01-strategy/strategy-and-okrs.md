# Product Strategy & OKRs: Meridian Foundations

> Module 1 · Write a Strategy That Can Survive a Test

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration** | Make Meridian a platform that provides reliable project visibility even when field reporting is incomplete. Enable superintendents and foremen to capture and report progress naturally within their daily workflows, while preserving Meridian's position as the enterprise system of record. |
| **Where to play** | Focus on improving field data capture and closing reporting gaps within existing large enterprise customers. Defer mid-market expansion and additional field workflows until data gaps are reduced to an acceptable and sustainable level. |
| **How to win** | Combine frontline simplicity with the enterprise traceability that standalone messaging tools lack. Deliver a mobile-first experience that supports natural field data capture while intelligently inferring missing information from available evidence, clearly distinguishing verified, inferred, and unknown data. |
| **Capabilities required** | Build excellence in rapid product iteration grounded in firsthand field observation, user research, and prototyping—not assumptions made from an office. Establish a strong field SME and champion network, strengthen mobile-first design, and develop data integration and data science capabilities for trustworthy inference. Extend the existing platform rather than rebuilding it. |
| **Management systems** | Track lagging indicators such as unresolved data gaps and projects with broken status chains. Review leading indicators weekly or biweekly, including adoption, repeat usage, direct data capture, task completion, and abandonment rates. Regularly evaluate the quality of both captured and inferred data, using hypothesis-driven experiments and formal investment decision gates at least quarterly. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> **Hard no:** We will not invest in expanding enterprise reporting capabilities or additional ERP integrations during the initial Foundations investment, unless they directly contribute to closing field data gaps. While commercially valuable, these enhancements would expand reporting capabilities without addressing the underlying problem of incomplete field data. We will also not pursue inference for its own sake. Any inferred information must meet high standards of accuracy, transparency, and trustworthiness. Otherwise, the teams consuming that information will not be able to rely on it for critical decisions.

## 3. OKR cascade

> **Objective:** Make Meridian the trusted source of project progress by embedding field reporting into daily workflows and providing reliable visibility even when reporting is incomplete.
>
> - **KR1:** Field Adoption:  Achieve 60% sustained weekly adoption among superintendents and foremen on pilot projects, defined as submitting field updates in at least 3 of 4 consecutive weeks for each month, within six months.
> - **KR2:** Data Gap Reduction:  Reduce the percentage of pilot projects with unresolved breaks in their status reporting chain by 40% from the initial baseline within six months, through improved direct data capture and validated inference.
> - **KR3:** Inference trust: Achieve 75% weekly usage of Meridian's verified or validated project-status information for operational decision-making among eligible pilot users, including project managers, superintendents, project engineers, and operations leaders, within six months (finance and financial decision-making are excluded from the initial pilot).

## 4. AI pressure-test

**Most valid challenge:** You're assuming that reducing friction will change field behavior. Your strategy assumes that if reporting becomes sufficiently simple and natural, superintendents and foremen will adopt Meridian instead of using their cameras and messaging apps. But what if the problem isn't usability? What if field teams don't perceive any personal value in structured reporting, regardless of how easy it becomes? A foreman gets immediate value from texting a photo to a subcontractor. Entering information into Meridian primarily benefits someone upstream. Your strategy explains how you'll make information easier to capture. It doesn't explain why a foreman would want to use Meridian tomorrow morning. If this assumption fails, your adoption strategy collapses, and inference becomes a workaround for a behavior problem you never solved.

**What I will change and what I will defend:** Change: Strengthen our How to Win strategy to prioritize immediate operational value for field workers, making data capture a natural byproduct of workflows they already find useful. Validate this through firsthand field research, rapid prototyping, and behavioral experiments before scaling. Defend: Continue focusing on existing enterprise customers, preserving the core system of record, and investing in trustworthy inference for unavoidable reporting gaps. We will not assume 100% field data capture, nor allow inference to conceal poor adoption or create false confidence.
